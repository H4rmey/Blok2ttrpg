# Invoking

Invoking is the way to use the traits to create a nerative. You can Invoke at any time even during combat. You do this by using Invoke points. Invoking can be done to primarily adjust skills for one roll. 

## Invoke points

Invoke points are the currency of the fiction. Every player has their own pool. Players cannot share invoke points. Points are not shared, not pooled with the party and not held by the GM on your behalf. 

Every character's invoke points return to their maximum at the start of every session or scenario. The pool is also refilled during play by letting your own traits work against you. By letting that happen, you earn a point. 

You can spend a point to do the following:
1) Invoke a trait to gain a bonus
2) Deny a invokation of another person
3) During combat: Gain an Action once per round (more on that later).
4) During combat: Choosing to fail the validation on purpose.
4) During combat: Forfeiting an action.

> NOTE: A GM or Player cannot be forced to spend a point. The invokation itself has to come from the Person playing the character. Denying it means that you did not agree with the invocation

## How to Invoke

Invoking can be done at almost any time, even during combat or after the dice have been rolled. You cannot invoke after the die rolls have been accepted however. Invoking can be done by both the GM and PC. Either party can refuse the invocation, this however will cost them one invoke point themselfs.

The person invoking has to elaborate why they are Invoking and how the trait fits the nerative at a given moment. 

## Invoking During Combat

During combat you can spend one invoke and two energy to gain a single action. This can be done at any time during combat, but only once per round. So this can also be done out of order, so it can act as a reaction. 

You can gain inoke points by skipping an action or failing a validation on purpose.

During combat, invocations can not denied!

## Advice for the GM

**Invoke against the players early.** Players are usually slow to volunteer complications, because it feels like arguing against themselves. Offering a couple of invocations in the first session teaches the table that traits are a currency rather than a decoration, and it puts points in their hands so they can start spending.

**Do not let the pool sit full.** A player who never spends is a player whose character sheet has stopped mattering. If the pool is untouched for a whole session, aim a complication at a trait they wrote down and see what happens.

**Say yes to strange invocations.** "Used to be a Detective" applying to a tavern brawl sounds like a stretch until the player explains they are reading the room for who throws the first punch. The stretch is the fun part.
